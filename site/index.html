The idea

You don't do one thing at a time. Your time tracker shouldn't pretend you do.

You write code, check the docs, answer a message and go back to the code. Most trackers file that as a long list of app switches. Weft finds the thread running through it: the same kind of work within 30 minutes becomes one stream, short detours fold in, and streams that overlap sit side by side like events in a calendar.

  1. 1

    Working time, not open sessions

    A turn runs from your prompt to the agent's reply. The hours a session sits waiting for you never count, and neither do its pauses for your permission or answers. The bar is solid where the agent worked and faint where it waited.

  2. 2

    Waiting on agents counts

    Step away while an agent works and that time still counts towards your daily target, up to a limit you set. Your Focus Score stays about you alone.

  3. 3

    Every machine you use

    With HAPI, agents on your other machines show up too, and sessions Weft can already read here are never counted twice.

Private by design

Your day stays on your Mac. All of it.

There's no account to create, no server to sync with and no analytics to opt out of. Weft categorizes, summarizes and reviews your day with Apple's on-device model, and keeps your history in a folder you can open in Finder.

What Weft records

  • The app in front
  • Its window title
  • The current tab's address, in supported browsers
  • When your coding agents worked

What it never records

  • Screenshots
  • Keystrokes
  • What's on your screen
  • Your prompts, or what agents reply

What you control

  • Stop recording window titles
  • Keep only the domain of each URL
  • Leave out apps and sites entirely
  • Pause any time, export everything to CSV

Prefer a different model? Connect any OpenAI-compatible API in Settings → Model: OpenAI, OpenRouter, DeepSeek and others. A remote model receives the apps, sites and window titles Weft asks it to sort, and your daily totals for reviews. Point Weft at Ollama or LM Studio and even that stays on your Mac.

Native, through and through

Feels like macOS. Barely touches your battery.

Weft is written in Swift and SwiftUI, so it looks, scrolls and behaves like the rest of your Mac. It doesn't poll your windows several times a second. It waits for macOS to say something changed, takes one look and goes back to sleep.

7MB

The whole app

No Electron and no bundled browser. The download is about 4 MB.

Event-driven

No polling loop. Weft wakes when the app, window or title changes, plus a quiet heartbeat once a minute.

Knows when you're away

It stops counting after 5 idle minutes, when the lid closes and when the screen locks. Meetings are the exception.

Install

  1. 1

    Download

    Get the latest Weft-<version>.dmg from GitHub Releases.

  2. 2

    Drag it into Applications

    It's signed and notarized by Apple, so macOS opens it without warnings.

  3. 3

    Allow Accessibility

    So the timeline can show which document or project you were in, not just the app.

Weft needs macOS 26 or later. Apple Intelligence is recommended but optional: without it, Weft still knows a few hundred apps and sites, and guesses the rest for you to confirm later. You can also connect an OpenAI-compatible API or a local Ollama.

Or build it yourself

You'll need Xcode and Homebrew.

$ brew install xcodegen
$ git clone https://github.com/tiann/weft.git
$ cd weft && xcodegen generate
$ xcodebuild -project Weft.xcodeproj \
    -scheme Weft -derivedDataPath build/DerivedData build

Bug reports and pull requests are welcome. AGENTS.md covers the architecture and the tests.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Is Weft really free?
Yes. Weft is MIT-licensed, with no subscription, no paid tier and no account. You can read the code, change it and build it yourself.
How does Weft measure my coding agents' time?
It reads the logs Claude Code and Codex keep on your Mac (~/.claude/projects and ~/.codex/sessions) and counts each turn, from your prompt to the agent's reply, minus the time it spent waiting for your permission or answers. The hours a session sits idle between prompts don't count. Today shows the time at least one agent was working and the total across sessions, and each session on the timeline shows its own working time. With HAPI 0.33 or later, agents on your other machines are included.
Do I need Apple Intelligence?
No, though it's recommended. Without it, Weft still sorts the apps and sites it knows and anything you've set yourself, and guesses the rest from each app's App Store category or each site's name. You can also connect any OpenAI-compatible API, including a local Ollama or LM Studio.
What exactly can Weft see?
The app in front, its window title and, in supported browsers, the current tab's address. Never screenshots, keystrokes or the contents of your screen. You can turn off window titles, keep only domains, and leave out apps and sites entirely.
Will it slow down my Mac or drain the battery?
It's built not to. Weft is a native app with no polling loop: it reacts when macOS reports that the app, window or title changed, and otherwise checks in once a minute.
How is Weft different from Rize?
Weft takes after Rize but starts from a different idea: people rarely do one thing at a time, so instead of a log of app switches it shows the threads of work you kept coming back to. It's also free, open source, and works entirely on your Mac.
Where is my data, and how do I delete it?
In ~/Library/Application Support/Weft/. Export it to CSV from Settings → Data. To erase it, quit Weft and delete that folder.
